Do I have to be behind on my mortgage to do a short sale?

Short answer

It depends on your lender. Some lenders require you to be at least one payment behind before they will consider a short sale. Others only need you to prove a hardship. If you are still current, keep making your payments until you know what your lender requires.

Why it depends

Each lender sets its own rules. Some will not look at a short sale until a payment has been missed. Others will review it while you are still current, as long as you can show a real hardship that means you cannot keep the home.

Do not stop paying to qualify

Keep making your payments. Missed payments are reported on your credit separately from the short sale, and they can lengthen the time before you can buy again.

Once you decide to work with us, we contact your lender and find out exactly what it requires. You make that decision knowing the facts, not guessing.

What the lender will want to see

  • A hardship. A change such as job loss, illness, divorce, or the death of a co-borrower. What counts as a hardship.
  • Documents that prove it. Lenders push back most often when documents are missing. We gather them with you.
  • A home worth less than you owe. If you could sell for enough to pay off the loan, you would not need the lender's approval.
