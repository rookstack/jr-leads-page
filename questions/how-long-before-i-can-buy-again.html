How long after a short sale can I buy a home again?

Short answer

Usually sooner than after a foreclosure. For a conventional loan, the standard wait is about 4 years after a short sale, compared with 7 years after a foreclosure. FHA loans generally ask for 3 years after either one. Your own lender can set a longer wait.

"Extenuating circumstances" means a one-time event outside your control, such as a serious illness or the death of the main earner, that you can document. The underwriter at your new lender decides whether your situation qualifies.

Why the short sale still matters

For a conventional loan, which is what most buyers use, the gap is three years. That is three years sooner that you can own a home again.

A foreclosure also becomes a public record. A short sale is a sale. The lender agrees to it, and the home changes hands like any other sale.

What can make your wait longer

  • Your lender's own rules. Agencies set the minimum. Individual lenders can ask for more.
  • Late payments before the sale. These show on your credit separately from the short sale itself.
  • Your credit after the sale. Lenders look at how you have handled credit since.
