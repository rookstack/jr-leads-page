Will the lender still come after me for the difference after a short sale?

Short answer

It depends on who owns your loan. Most loans we see are owned by Fannie Mae or Freddie Mac, and those waive the remaining balance as part of the short sale approval. FHA, VA and USDA loans usually do too, but no lender guarantees it. You see the terms before anything is final, and if the difference is not waived, you can decide not to go ahead.

What "the difference" means

In a short sale, the home sells for less than you owe. The gap between the two is called the deficiency.

For example, if you owe $300,000 and the home sells for $250,000, the deficiency is $50,000. The question is whether the lender forgives that $50,000 or keeps the right to collect it from you later.

Who owns your loan matters most

The company you send payments to is not always the company that owns your loan. Many loans are owned by Fannie Mae or Freddie Mac and only serviced by your lender.

  • Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac loans. The remaining balance is waived as part of the short sale approval. Most of the short sales we handle are these loans.
  • FHA, VA and USDA loans. These usually waive it too, but it is not guaranteed.
  • Other lenders. A waiver is possible, but it is decided case by case.

Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac each have a free loan lookup on their websites where you can check whether they own your loan.

You see the terms before anything is final

When the lender approves the short sale, it sends approval documents that set the terms of the sale, including whether the deficiency is waived. Nothing closes until you have seen them.

If the lender does not waive the difference, you do not have to go ahead with the sale.

If you have a second mortgage or home equity line

Each lender on the home has to agree to the short sale. A second lender usually wants a payment at closing before it will release its lien, and who covers that payment varies from one sale to the next.

If you have a second mortgage or a home equity line, tell us at the start. It changes the picture, and you should know that before anything else.
