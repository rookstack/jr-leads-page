Can I still do a short sale if foreclosure has already started?

Short answer

Often, yes, but the earlier the better. Once a foreclosure sale date has been set, it is difficult to change, and postponing it usually means hiring an attorney, at a cost. If foreclosure has started, talk to someone before a sale date is set.

Why the sale date matters most

A short sale needs a buyer, an offer, and the lender's review and approval. Each of those takes time. Before a sale date is set, there is room for all of it.

After the court has issued a final judgment and set a sale date, the sale can sometimes still be postponed, but that usually means an attorney and fees, and it is decided case by case.

If a date for the foreclosure sale has been set, find it before you do anything else. It is on the court papers or the notice you received.

How foreclosure works here

In Florida, foreclosure goes through the courts. The lender files a lawsuit, and the home is only sold after the court allows it.

The lawsuit and the short sale are separate

Working on a short sale does not answer the foreclosure lawsuit. If you have received court papers, they have deadlines of their own. A foreclosure attorney can tell you your options in the case itself.

What to have ready when you call

  • Any foreclosure notice or court papers you have received
  • The sale date, if one has been set
  • Your most recent mortgage statement
  • The name of your lender, and of any second lender
