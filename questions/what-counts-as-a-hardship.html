What counts as a hardship for a short sale?

Short answer

A hardship is a change in your life that means you can no longer afford the mortgage, now or soon. Common examples are job loss or reduced income, illness or medical bills, divorce, and the death of a borrower or the main earner.

Common hardships lenders accept

  • Job loss, or a cut in income or hours
  • Illness, disability, or medical bills
  • Divorce or separation
  • The death of a co-borrower, or of the main earner in the household
  • A military move or a job transfer
  • A payment increase on an adjustable-rate loan
  • A business that failed or lost income
  • Everyday costs that have risen beyond what your income covers

It can be short term or long term

The question is whether you can keep paying. A hardship that has already happened counts, and so can one you can see coming, such as a confirmed layoff date.

Missing documents are the most common problem

Lenders rarely push back because a hardship is not real. They push back because the documents that prove it are missing. Once you decide to work with us, we gather them with you so nothing is left out.

What you will need to show

  • A hardship letter. Keep it short and stick to the facts: what changed, and when. It does not need to be formal.
  • Proof of income and expenses. Usually pay stubs, bank statements and tax returns.
  • Proof of the event, where there is one. For example a termination letter, medical bills or records, a divorce decree, a death certificate, military orders, a notice of a payment increase, or business tax returns and statements.
